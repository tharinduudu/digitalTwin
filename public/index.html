Digital TwinA window into the invisible.

High-energy particles from space strike Earth’s atmosphere, creating muons that pass through us. When a muon crosses our detector’s tiles, it can leave tiny flashes of light—signals that help us detect and count these invisible visitors.

What is real here, and what is illustrated?

The frame profiles, tile, holder and enclosure derive from supplied CAD. The readout PCB comes from KiCad; the Raspberry Pi 5 uses manufacturer CAD. Carrier boards, connectors and some fasteners are illustrative. Acrylic and liner thicknesses are assumed.

This guided track is a selected 4 GeV example through the reference detector, calculated with the existing muon transport model. It is not a measured event or a random sample of the sky. Flight plays at 2 ns per displayed second. Green fibre light and red indicators are teaching animations, not photon transport, measured timing or FPGA logic.

The full count simulation uses a sea-level source, approximate transport and ideal detection. Indoor shielding, optical efficiency, thresholds and electronic noise are not included in those counts.
