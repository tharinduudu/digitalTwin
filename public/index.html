About this display

A particle, a flash, a count.

A charged particle crossing a plastic scintillator can produce light. A silicon photomultiplier converts that light into an electrical pulse. Pulses in two tiles form a coincidence.

Muon source and counts

Simulation uses the energy-dependent sea-level muon spectrum of Guan et al., with kinetic energies from 0.1 GeV to 10 TeV. Arrival directions, boundary entry positions and Poisson arrival times are sampled from its absolute flux. The rate is not adjusted to 230 counts/min.

Muons enter through the top and sides of the surrounding volume. Hits are checked against the full 1 cm tile thickness and the cut corner in the supplied tile model. A coincidence requires the same simulated muon to deposit energy in both tiles. A triple contributes to all three pair counts; adding the pair totals double-counts particles.

Motion and material

Muon speeds follow relativistic kinematics. Mean energy loss and stopping use PDG polystyrene data. Deflections use a Gaussian, thin-slab approximation to multiple scattering, and muons can decay. Playback shows 2 ns of particle flight per second. Light propagation and red LED flashes are slowed illustrations, not an optical or electronics simulation.

What is not known here

This is an open-sky, sea-level model with ideal detection. It does not predict the measured response of this indoor detector: roof shielding, local altitude and weather, passive housing materials, secondary particles, energy-loss fluctuations, SiPM efficiency, thresholds and electronic noise are not included. The Gaussian scattering core does not reproduce rare large-angle tails. It is not a Geant4 transport calculation.

Unrelated room tracks are thinned for drawing without changing any count. The yellow overlay is the straight-line acceptance from one lower-tile centre to the upper tile outline, not the acceptance averaged over the whole detector.

Live detector mode

When connected locally, counts come from the detector's completed minute log. Simulated counts never replace missing measurements. Drawn muons and LED timing remain illustrations and are not added to measured totals.
