Calculation, uncertainties and a fair comparison

The worker samples the bundled Guan et al. sea-level energy–angle source (0.1 GeV–10 TeV) through all incoming faces of a fixed 64 cm world cube. Rays transform into detector coordinates before the existing finite-volume transport: cut corners, PDG polystyrene mean energy loss, stopping, decay and approximate multiple scattering. The same 20 × 20 × 1 cm tiles and 13 cm centre spacing are used at every angle. The instrument rotates; the sky stays fixed.

Rates are Monte Carlo estimates with numerical standard errors. Long exposures use independent Poisson draws for mutually exclusive tile-hit patterns, then reconstruct overlapping pair and triple counts. These are hypothetical stationary observations, not historical dates or predictions of changing weather. An exposure lasts exactly 7, 30, 90 or 180 days here.

Detection is ideal: no building, ground, frame or housing shielding; no altitude/latitude correction, thresholds, photon efficiency, noise, accidental coincidences or dead time. Compass rotation cannot demonstrate a real geomagnetic asymmetry with this azimuthally symmetric source. Changing compass bearing alone should agree within numerical sampling error for this symmetric sky. Numerical errors are not physical accuracy claims.

For a fair experiment, hold exposure and site fixed, change one setting, and repeat. Counts at different durations must be divided by exposure time.
