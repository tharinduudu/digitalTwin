Go deeper: what the diagram represents

Scintillation is molecular photophysics. A passing charged particle loses energy mainly through electromagnetic interactions with electrons. Only a fraction appears as scintillation light. Organic plastic scintillators commonly use fluorescent additives and energy transfer in a polymer matrix. The particular fluor formulation of this supplied tile has not been established here.

The energy diagram shows a conceptual ground and excited electronic manifold with vibrational relaxation. It is not an electron orbit picture, an atomic spectrum, or measured energy levels for this tile. Relaxation can release heat; radiative transitions produce photons. Not every excitation leads to a detected photon.

The wavelength-shifting fibre absorbs some light and re-emits at a longer wavelength. Only a fraction is trapped and transported; reflection, absorption, attenuation and imperfect coupling cause losses. Green packets indicate the teaching convention used in the detector view; no measured emission spectrum, refractive index, attenuation length or timing is asserted.

The Hamamatsu S13360-2050VE is the reference sensor. An absorbed photon can trigger a Geiger avalanche in a biased microcell. Photon detection probability, recovery, crosstalk, afterpulsing and dark noise need their own measured or manufacturer-supported model. The illustrated pulse here is conceptual. The actual-board electronics bench supplies the separate reduced-order electrical model.

The energy calculation uses the existing PDG polystyrene stopping/range tables and reference tile volume. It uses mean energy loss, not sampled Landau/Vavilov fluctuations, optical transport or a validated scintillation yield. Continue to the detector-angle experiment →

Background reading: Hamamatsu: physics and operation of the MPPC; Eljen: organic scintillators and wavelength shifting materials. These explain general principles; they do not identify or calibrate the supplied tile or fibre.
